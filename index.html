Reel One — The Story

The maverick of the Golden Age

Bob Chinn arrived in filmmaking the long way around. Born in Hawaii in 1943 to Chinese immigrant parents who later settled in California, he studied at Santa Monica College before earning a degree from the UCLA film school in 1966 — the same program, in the same era, that produced future Doors frontman Jim Morrison, a classmate of his.

Hollywood proper proved hard to crack. To support a young family, Chinn took the work that was available in the early 1970s, and in doing so walked straight into the so-called Golden Age of adult cinema — a brief window when erotic features were shot on film, played theatrically, and were reviewed in the press.

He gave adult film something it had never had: a hero who came back for the next picture.

His instinct was narrative. Where the genre traded in disconnected scenes, Chinn built a recurring character — the hard-boiled private eye Johnny Wadd — and a serialized franchise around him, an idea borrowed straight from the film-noir detective serials he loved. It changed the commercial logic of the form, and it made a star of the actor he cast in the part.

Reel Two — The Career

A working life, by the year

1966

Out of UCLA

Graduates from the UCLA film school with ambitions of mainstream directing.

~1970

Into the trade

Begins taking directing work in the burgeoning adult-film business to support his family.

1971

Johnny Wadd is born

Casts a tall newcomer named John C. Holmes as a noir private detective and launches the series that defines his career.

1971–78

The nine-film run

Directs the complete original Johnny Wadd cycle, the first recurring-character franchise of its kind.

1977–80

Peak output

Helms a string of theatrical features — The Jade Pussycat, Candy Stripers, Hot & Saucy Pizza Girls, Prisoner of Paradise — at the height of the Golden Age.

1980s–90s

The author years

Steps back from regular directing; later writes a memoir and a Johnny Wadd novel, and is sought out as a chronicler of the era.

2001–02

The revival

Returns to the chair for a new four-film Johnny Wadd run, recasting the role for a new generation.

Reel Five — The Legacy

Beyond the screen

On Screen

The Boogie Nights connection

Chinn is widely cited as an inspiration for Burt Reynolds’ director character, Jack Horner, in Paul Thomas Anderson’s Boogie Nights (1997).

Documentary

Telling the real story

He was interviewed extensively for Wadd: The Life & Times of John C. Holmes (1999), the definitive film record of the star he discovered.

In Print

The author

Chinn turned chronicler — publishing the memoir The Other Side of Paradise and the crime novel Flesh of the Lotus: A Johnny Wadd Novel.

The Innovation

A franchise first

By building recurring characters into adult features, Chinn is credited with fundamentally reshaping how the genre told — and sold — its stories.
